# Marvel Multiverse Roleplaying Game — Occupations Reference

> **Player-facing reference**
>
> This document summarizes the Occupations in the *Marvel Multiverse Role-Playing Game* in original wording. Occupations represent what a character does or recently did for a living and grant a package of Traits and Tags at no additional character-building cost.
>
> The Core Rulebook explicitly treats its occupation list as non-exhaustive: players and Narrators may create or tailor occupations. This file covers the standard Core Rulebook occupations commonly listed in the official/basic rules. It does not claim that every later adventure, expansion, or third-party supplement introduces additional occupations.
>
> Consult the rulebook and current errata for authoritative wording and edge cases.

## How Occupations work

- A character normally begins with **one Occupation**.
- An Occupation grants its listed **Traits** and **Tags** for free.
- Traits have rules effects. Tags are primarily narrative descriptors and usually do not change a roll by themselves.
- A character may gain another Occupation during play at the Narrator's discretion.
- A character may lose an Occupation during play if the story supports it.
- Occupations do **not** normally add points directly to Melee, Agility, Resilience, Vigilance, Ego, or Logic. Their benefits are delivered through Traits and Tags.
- The **Extra Occupation** Trait can provide another Occupation and its associated benefits.

## Quick rules glossary

| Term | Player meaning |
|---|---|
| **Trait** | A rules-facing benefit, permission, or drawback. |
| **Tag** | A descriptive label used to support roleplay, story permissions, and character identity. |
| **Edge** | Roll an additional die and keep the better result, following the core rules. |
| **Trouble** | Roll an additional die and keep the worse result, following the core rules. |
| **Connections** | Lets the character seek assistance, information, or favors from a defined group, using the Trait’s rules. |
| **Presence** | Improves the character’s ability to influence or command attention, using the Trait’s rules. |
| **Font of Information** | Represents broad access to useful knowledge and information sources. |

# Occupations

## Adventurer

**Description:** The character makes a living—or spends most of their time—exploring dangerous places, seeking treasure, investigating mysteries, or taking on hazardous jobs.

**Tags**

- **Black Market Access:** The character has access to illicit or unusual markets, contacts, or suppliers.

**Traits**

- **Fearless:** The character is unusually resistant to fear and intimidation; use the Trait’s rules when a fear-related effect or check applies.
- **Connections: Super Heroes or Villains:** The character can seek favors, information, or assistance from super-powered contacts.

**What this means in play**

An Adventurer is good at getting into places ordinary people avoid and knows people in the superhuman world. The Occupation does not automatically grant combat Powers or a numerical bonus to an Ability Score.

---

## Assassin

**Description:** The character was trained or employed to eliminate targets discreetly and efficiently.

**Tags**

- **Streetwise:** The character understands criminal and urban networks.
- **Villainous:** Commonly appropriate, but this Tag is not mandatory if the character’s history does not support it.

**Traits**

- **Connections: Criminal:** The character can seek help, information, or resources from criminal contacts.
- **Signature Attack:** The character has a distinctive attack or technique with the mechanical benefits described by the Trait.

**What this means in play**

An Assassin excels at planning, choosing targets, and using a trademark attack. Signature Attack is a Trait benefit; it is not itself a Power Set.

---

## Criminal

**Description:** The character has a history of theft, organized crime, smuggling, burglary, or another illegal trade.

**Tags**

- **Black Market Access**
- **Streetwise**

**Traits**

- **Connections: Criminal:** The character can call on criminal contacts for information, favors, equipment, or introductions, subject to the Narrator’s judgment.

**What this means in play**

Criminal provides access to the underworld and illicit resources. It does not automatically make the character a villain or grant a combat bonus.

---

## Educator

**Description:** The character teaches, mentors, trains, or otherwise helps others learn.

**Traits**

- **Connections: Community:** The character can seek assistance from people in a community.
- **Font of Information:** The character has broad knowledge and useful information sources.
- **Presence:** The character is skilled at commanding attention, teaching, or influencing a group.

**What this means in play**

Educators are valuable in investigation, research, mentoring, public speaking, and community scenes. The Occupation does not directly increase Logic or Ego; its Traits provide the relevant benefits.

---

## Engineer

**Description:** The character designs, builds, repairs, or maintains machines, structures, devices, or advanced technology.

**Tags**

- **Lab Access:** The character has access to a laboratory, workshop, or technical facility.

**Traits**

- **Gearhead:** The character is especially capable with machines, devices, and technology.
- **Inventor:** The character can create or improvise devices and technological solutions under the Trait’s rules.

**What this means in play**

Engineer is ideal for characters who build equipment, maintain vehicles, repair systems, or solve technical problems. It does not automatically grant High Tech as an Origin or any specific technological Power.

---

## Entertainer

**Description:** The character works as a performer, actor, musician, comedian, celebrity, or other public entertainer.

**Traits**

- **Famous:** The character is recognized by some audience or public.
- **Presence:** The character can command attention and influence an audience.
- **Public Speaking:** The character is skilled at addressing groups and communicating clearly in public.

**What this means in play**

Entertainers are effective in social scenes, public appearances, performances, and situations where fame or stage presence matters. Famous can also create complications when recognition is inconvenient.

---

## Health Care Worker

**Description:** The character works in medicine, nursing, emergency care, therapy, or another health-care profession.

**Traits**

- **Clinician:** The character is trained in diagnosis, treatment, and medical knowledge.
- **First Aid:** The character can provide emergency medical assistance using the Trait’s rules.

**What this means in play**

Health Care Worker is useful for stabilizing injured characters, diagnosing conditions, treating patients, and operating in hospitals or emergency settings. It does not grant a healing Power by itself.

---

## Investigator

**Description:** The character investigates crimes, mysteries, missing persons, conspiracies, or unusual events.

**Traits**

- **Connections: Police:** The character can seek information or assistance from law-enforcement contacts.
- **Interrogation:** The character is skilled at questioning people and extracting information.
- **Investigation:** The character is trained to find clues, reconstruct events, and follow leads.

**What this means in play**

Investigator supports clue gathering and interviews. The Traits give permission and advantages in appropriate scenes; they do not guarantee that every clue is found without a check or Narrator ruling.

---

## Journalist

**Description:** The character reports news, writes articles, broadcasts stories, or investigates matters for a publication or audience.

**Traits**

- **Audience:** The character has a group of people who follow or pay attention to their work.
- **Connections: Sources:** The character can seek information from informants and sources.
- **Pundit:** The character is skilled at analyzing events and presenting informed commentary.

**What this means in play**

Journalists are good at finding sources, publishing information, shaping public discussion, and leveraging an audience. Audience and Connections can also create story complications involving deadlines, public scrutiny, or source protection.

---

## Law Enforcer

**Description:** The character works in law enforcement, security, investigations, corrections, or another official enforcement role.

**Tags**

- **Authority:** The character has some official or institutional authority.
- **Backup:** The character may be able to call on colleagues or official support.

**Traits**

- **Interrogation:** The character is trained to question people and obtain information.
- **Investigation:** The character is trained to find clues and reconstruct events.

**What this means in play**

Law Enforcer is useful when dealing with police procedure, official access, investigations, and backup. Authority and Backup are not unlimited powers: the Narrator decides whether the character’s agency, jurisdiction, and circumstances allow assistance.

---

## Lawyer

**Description:** The character practices law, provides legal advice, represents clients, or works in legal advocacy.

**Traits**

- **Dealmaker:** The character is skilled at negotiating agreements and making deals.
- **Legal Eagle:** The character understands legal systems, rights, procedures, and arguments.
- **Public Speaking:** The character is skilled at presenting arguments to groups or formal audiences.

**What this means in play**

Lawyer supports negotiation, legal research, courtroom scenes, contracts, and disputes. It does not automatically allow the character to bypass laws or compel someone to agree.

---

## Leader

**Description:** The character commands a team, organization, movement, faction, or community.

**Tags**

- **Authority:** The character has recognized authority.
- **Powerful:** The character is regarded as influential or important.

**Traits**

- **Presence:** The character can command attention, motivate others, and influence groups.

**What this means in play**

Leader is ideal for team captains, heads of organizations, political figures, and commanders. The Tags establish social standing; Presence supplies the mechanical benefit.

---

## Military

**Description:** The character served in an armed force, military organization, or comparable combat unit.

**Traits**

- **Battle Ready:** The character has increased readiness and battlefield endurance; apply the Trait’s listed Focus benefit.
- **Connections: Military:** The character can seek assistance, information, or resources from military contacts.
- **Situational Awareness:** The character is skilled at noticing threats, reading a battlefield, and recognizing tactical danger.

**Tags**

- **Obligation: Duty** is commonly appropriate for active-duty characters, though it is a Tag rather than an automatic mechanical penalty.

**What this means in play**

Military supports combat readiness, tactical awareness, and access to military contacts. Battle Ready is the important direct resource benefit; it does not increase an Ability Score.

---

## Outsider

**Description:** The character comes from another planet, dimension, time period, culture, or society and is unfamiliar with the campaign’s ordinary world.

**Traits**

- **Connections: Outsiders:** The character can seek assistance from other outsiders or unusual communities.
- **Fresh Eyes:** The character brings an unconventional perspective and may notice things locals overlook.
- **Stranger:** The character is unfamiliar with local customs, institutions, or expectations.

**What this means in play**

Outsider is useful for characters such as aliens, time travelers, dimensional travelers, and newly arrived heroes. Fresh Eyes is a useful investigative and roleplaying benefit, while Stranger can create social complications.

---

## Scientist

**Description:** The character conducts research, performs experiments, develops theories, or works in a scientific field.

**Tags**

- **Lab Access:** The character has access to a laboratory or research facility.

**Traits**

- **Inventor:** The character can create or improvise devices and technological solutions under the Trait’s rules.
- **Scientific Expertise:** The character has specialized scientific knowledge and can apply it to research and problem-solving.

**What this means in play**

Scientist is ideal for researchers, inventors, doctors who conduct research, and experimentalists. It does not automatically grant Weird Science or High Tech as an Origin.

---

## Spy

**Description:** The character works in espionage, intelligence, covert operations, infiltration, or clandestine information gathering.

**Tags**

- **Black Market Access:** The character has access to illicit suppliers and underworld contacts.

**Traits**

- **Connections: Espionage:** The character can seek information, equipment, or assistance from intelligence contacts.
- **Leverage:** The character knows how to use secrets, favors, and compromising information to influence others.

**What this means in play**

Spy supports infiltration, intelligence gathering, covert contacts, and social manipulation. Leverage is strongest when the character actually has useful information or influence over the target.

---

## Student

**Description:** The character attends school, university, a training program, or another educational institution.

**Tags**

- **Mentor:** The character has a teacher, guide, or experienced person who can provide advice.
- **Obligation: School:** The character has responsibilities connected to education.

**Traits**

- **Quick Learner:** The character learns new skills and information rapidly under the Trait’s rules.

**What this means in play**

Student is a good fit for younger heroes and characters still in formal training. Mentor and Obligation create story hooks; Quick Learner is the direct mechanical benefit.

# Occupation selection checklist

When selecting an Occupation:

1. Choose the Occupation that best describes the character’s current life or recent past.
2. Record every listed Trait.
3. Record every listed Tag.
4. Check whether a Tag creates a story obligation, vulnerability, or source of contacts.
5. Do not add direct Ability Score points unless a specific Trait says to do so.
6. Ask the Narrator how Contacts, Authority, Audience, Backup, Lab Access, and other setting-dependent benefits work in the campaign.
7. If the character has changed careers, discuss whether they should gain or lose an Occupation during play.

# Creating a custom Occupation

The rules allow custom Occupations. A balanced custom Occupation should usually provide:

- One or two thematic Tags.
- Two or three Traits that describe the character’s work.
- No automatic superhuman Power unless the Narrator deliberately creates a special exception.
- Benefits that are useful in appropriate scenes rather than universally applicable to every roll.

Examples:

- **Firefighter:** Authority or Backup; First Aid; Situational Awareness.
- **Mechanic:** Lab Access; Gearhead; Inventor.
- **Professor:** Connections: Community; Font of Information; Scientific Expertise.
- **Detective:** Connections: Police; Investigation; Interrogation.
- **Paramedic:** Clinician; First Aid; Connections: Community.
