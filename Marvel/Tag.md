# Marvel Multiverse Roleplaying Game — Tags Reference

> **Player-facing reference**
>
> Tags are primarily narrative labels. Unlike Traits, they generally do **not** provide a numerical modifier, bonus die, penalty, or direct combat effect by themselves. They describe facts about a character—identity, background, relationships, resources, vulnerabilities, appearance, or circumstances.
>
> This reference summarizes the published Tag system in original wording. It is not a reproduction of the rulebooks. Consult the relevant rulebook and current errata for authoritative wording.

## How Tags work

- A Tag describes an aspect of the character.
- Tags normally have **no inherent mechanical modifier**.
- There is **no general limit** to the number of Tags a character can have.
- Tags can be gained, changed, or removed during play.
- A Tag can matter mechanically when another rule explicitly references it.
- Tags can also create narrative complications.
- If you are looking for something that directly changes an Ability Score or grants an Edge/other roll modifier, it is usually a **Trait**, not a Tag.

The official Basic Rules explicitly distinguish Traits, which generally have mechanical effects, from Tags, which primarily affect the narrative. citeturn0search0turn2view0

# Core Rulebook Tags

## A.I.

**Description:** The character is an artificial intelligence or has copies of their mind stored elsewhere.

**Mechanics:** No inherent numerical modifier. The Tag establishes the character's unusual relationship with computers, bodies, and stored consciousness.

---

## Alien Heritage

**Description:** The character is extraterrestrial or has significant alien ancestry.

**Mechanics:** No inherent modifier. Establishes alien cultural knowledge, ancestry, and related narrative permissions.

---

## Alternate Form: [form]

**Description:** The character has a distinct alternate physical form.

**Mechanics:** The Tag identifies which character features belong to a particular form. The actual Traits, Powers, Size, and other rules provide the mechanical benefits. The Tag itself does not grant them.

---

## Amphibious

**Description:** The character is naturally adapted to underwater environments.

**Mechanics:** The character can breathe underwater and can see and hear normally while underwater.

---

## Auditory Issues

**Description:** The character has impaired hearing, ranging from partial hearing loss to deafness.

**Mechanics:** No universal numerical modifier. Hearing-dependent actions can be affected according to the character's actual impairment and the Narrator's ruling.

---

## Authority

**Description:** The character possesses recognized legal or institutional authority.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The Tag can provide narrative access to official facilities, information, personnel, or procedures appropriate to that authority.

---

## Backup

**Description:** The character belongs to an organization that can provide assistance.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The character can call for immediate organizational backup when circumstances permit. The Narrator determines what help can arrive and how quickly.

---

## Black Market Access

**Description:** The character knows how to buy and sell rare, restricted, or illegal goods.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Provides narrative access to black-market contacts and transactions.

---

## Chaotic

**Description:** The character is connected to primal or cosmic chaos.

**Mechanics:** No inherent modifier. Establishes a source or association with chaos for narrative purposes.

---

## Convict

**Description:** The character has a known criminal conviction or prison record.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Can affect employment, reputation, legal treatment, and social interactions.

---

## Cursed

**Description:** The character is affected by or associated with a supernatural curse.

**Mechanics:** No universal modifier. Any actual curse effects come from the specific character concept, Trait, Power, or rule that defines the curse.

---

## Deceased

**Description:** The character is technically dead or is legally recorded as deceased.

**Mechanics:** No inherent combat modifier. Creates unusual legal, identity, and social circumstances.

---

## Dependents

**Description:** Other people rely on the character for care, protection, money, or support.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Creates an ongoing responsibility and possible story complications.

---

## Enemy: [enemy]

**Description:** A named person, organization, or group actively opposes the character.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Establishes an antagonist who can pursue the character or interfere with their goals.

---

## Eternally Immortal

**Description:** The character does not age normally and has an unusual form of immortality.

**Mechanics:** No normal attack or defense bonus. The Tag establishes the character's unusual relationship with aging and death; specific resurrection/restoration rules depend on the character and campaign.

---

## Extreme Appearance

**Description:** The character has an obviously unusual appearance that is difficult to disguise.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Makes blending into ordinary society or concealing the character's identity more difficult.

---

## Headquarters

**Description:** The character has a dedicated base of operations.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Provides a location for planning, resting, storing equipment, meeting allies, and other appropriate activities. Specific facilities must be established with the Narrator.

---

## Heroic

**Description:** The character is recognized as a hero or is strongly associated with heroic behavior.

**Mechanics:** No inherent modifier. Primarily establishes reputation and narrative positioning.

---

## Hounded

**Description:** The character is actively persecuted or harassed because of who or what they are.

**Mechanics:** No flat penalty. The Narrator can use the Tag to introduce pursuit, prejudice, surveillance, legal problems, or similar complications.

---

## Hunted

**Description:** Someone is actively searching for the character.

**Mechanics:** No flat penalty. Establishes an ongoing threat or pursuit.

---

## Inhuman Genes

**Description:** The character possesses Inhuman genetic heritage.

**Mechanics:** No inherent modifier. Establishes ancestry and supports appropriate Inhuman-related character concepts.

---

## Lab Access

**Description:** The character can use a laboratory, workshop, or scientific facility.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Provides narrative permission to conduct research, experimentation, analysis, and technological work when the facility is appropriate.

---

## Linguist: [language]

**Description:** The character speaks a language other than their native language.

**Mechanics:** The character can communicate in the selected language. This Tag can be taken multiple times for different languages.

---

## Lunar Transformation

**Description:** The character transforms into a werewolf during specified full-moon periods.

**Mechanics:** During the specified period, the character must transform and follows the werewolf form's rules and animal nature. The Tag itself does not supply a separate numerical bonus.

---

## Mentor

**Description:** The character has an experienced teacher, guide, or role model.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The Mentor can provide advice, training, information, introductions, or other assistance as appropriate.

---

## Mental Health Conditions

**Description:** Represents a mental-health condition as part of the character's identity or history.

**Mechanics:** No inherent penalty or Ability adjustment. The X-Men Expansion uses this kind of Tag to represent character facts without automatically turning them into numerical disadvantages.

---

## Mobility Issues

**Description:** Represents a mobility-related condition or circumstance.

**Mechanics:** No inherent Speed, Agility, or Ability adjustment. Any specific effect should be defined with the player and Narrator rather than assumed from the Tag alone.

---

## Mysterious

**Description:** The character's origin or source of powers is unknown or poorly understood.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Creates a mystery that can be explored through the campaign.

---

## Obligation: [obligation]

**Description:** The character has a person, group, organization, place, or duty that depends on them.

**Mechanics:** No flat modifier. The Narrator can invoke the obligation as a responsibility, complication, or motivation.

---

## Poor

**Description:** The character has very limited financial resources.

**Mechanics:** No direct Ability adjustment in the Core rules. The Tag limits the character's ordinary access to money and expensive resources.

**Expansion interaction:** Later Spider-Verse rules can give Tags such as Poor more explicit consequences in particular situations. Apply those only when that optional expansion rule is being used.

---

## Powerful

**Description:** The character has substantial social, political, organizational, or community influence.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Can justify access to resources, people, facilities, or information associated with the character's position.

---

## Public Identity

**Description:** The character's superhero identity is publicly known.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The character can openly seek assistance, but enemies can more easily identify and target people associated with them.

---

## Radioactive

**Description:** The character constantly emits low-level radiation.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Appropriate detection equipment, such as a Geiger counter, can detect the radiation.

---

## Rich

**Description:** The character has substantial financial resources.

**Mechanics:** No Ability adjustment. The character can generally buy things that are normally and legally available without ordinary affordability being a significant obstacle.

---

## Secret Identity

**Description:** The character's true identity is hidden from the public.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The character must take precautions to preserve the secret, protecting friends and family while creating complications if the secret is threatened.

---

## Secondary Mutation

**Description:** A mutant has developed an additional ability outside their original specialization.

**Mechanics:** No direct Ability adjustment.

**X-Men Expansion interaction:** The Tag can permit a mutant to exceed the normal Rank Cap when selecting Powers unrelated to their existing Power Sets, subject to the expansion's rules.

---

## Signature Weapon: [weapon]

**Description:** The character is especially associated with a particular weapon.

**Mechanics:** No inherent attack bonus. A separate Trait, **Signature Attack**, can provide an Edge for attacks made with a chosen weapon or attack type.

**Important distinction:** `Signature Weapon` is a Tag; `Signature Attack` is a Trait.

---

## Sorcerous

**Description:** The character has studied traditional magic.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Establishes magical training and narrative permission involving sorcery.

---

## Streetwise

**Description:** The character understands criminal enterprises, street culture, and underworld contacts.

**Mechanics:** No inherent Edge. Provides useful narrative knowledge when dealing with gangs, criminals, illicit markets, and similar groups.

---

## Supernatural

**Description:** The character's abilities have a magical or supernatural basis.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Establishes the nature of the character's powers.

---

## Unusual Size: [size]

**Description:** The character has a size substantially outside the ordinary human range.

**Mechanics:** The Tag describes the unusual size; actual Size rules provide the mechanical consequences.

---

## Villainous

**Description:** The character is willing to disregard the rights, safety, or lives of others to achieve their goals.

**Mechanics:** No inherent modifier. Other rules can use the Tag as an eligibility condition for villain-focused mechanics.

---

## Vision Issues

**Description:** The character has impaired vision, ranging from limited sight to blindness.

**Mechanics:** No universal numerical modifier. Sight-dependent actions are affected according to the actual impairment and applicable visibility rules.

---

## Worshipped

**Description:** Other people regard the character as a god or divine figure.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. Establishes religious/social status and can justify worshippers, temples, followers, or related story resources.

---

## X-Gene

**Description:** The character possesses the mutant gene associated with mutant abilities.

**Mechanics:** No inherent Ability or Power bonus. Establishes mutant identity and can qualify the character for rules that require the X-Gene.

---

## Young

**Description:** The character is under eighteen.

**Mechanics:** No numerical modifier. The character may lack adult legal rights and may face restrictions on movement, employment, resources, or other adult activities.

---

# X-Men Expansion: Tag interactions

The X-Men Expansion adds Tags and also introduces rules that can explicitly reference Tags. Reviews of the expansion identify **Mental Health Conditions, Mobility Issues, Secondary Mutation, and Mutant Associate** among the additional Tag material. citeturn4search0

## Mutant Associate

**Description:** A non-mutant character who is associated with mutant communities or causes.

**Mechanics:** No general numerical modifier.

**Mutant Reputation interaction:** The Tag can matter under the X-Men Expansion's Mutant Reputation rules when determining how a non-mutant character is regarded in relation to mutants.

# Tags that interact with expansion rules

## Alternate Form

The Spider-Verse Expansion adds additional functionality around Alternate Form. When those rules are used, Traits, Tags, and Powers can be assigned to a particular form, allowing the character sheet to switch which features are active when the character transforms. citeturn5search3

## Poor

The Spider-Verse Expansion's optional rules can give the Poor Tag additional mechanical consequences, particularly when the character needs resources for superhero equipment or technology. These effects should **not** be applied to a Core-only game. citeturn5search0

## Villainous

The Spider-Verse Expansion's optional **Sinister Plots** rules use Villainous characters as an eligibility condition. The Villainous Tag itself does not automatically grant the associated resource. citeturn5search3

## Secondary Mutation

The X-Men Expansion specifically gives Secondary Mutation a character-building function: it can allow a mutant to exceed the normal Rank Cap for Powers outside their existing Power Sets, subject to the expansion's restrictions. citeturn4search0

# Tags vs. Traits — quick reference

| Tag | What it does | Does it directly modify a roll/stat? |
|---|---|---|
| Signature Weapon | Establishes an iconic weapon | **No** |
| Rich | Establishes substantial wealth | **No** |
| Poor | Establishes limited wealth | **No** in Core |
| Lab Access | Provides access to a laboratory | **No** |
| Authority | Establishes legal/institutional authority | **No** |
| Streetwise | Establishes criminal/street knowledge | **No** |
| Secret Identity | Establishes a hidden civilian identity | **No** |
| Public Identity | Establishes a known identity | **No** |
| Heroic | Establishes heroic reputation | **No** |
| Villainous | Establishes villainous outlook | **No** |
| X-Gene | Establishes mutant heritage | **No** |
| Supernatural | Establishes magical/supernatural source | **No** |
| Alternate Form | Identifies a separate character form | **No**, but expansion rules can activate/deactivate features |
| Secondary Mutation | Establishes an additional mutant development | **No**, but X-Men rules can change Power-selection limits |

# Commonly confused Traits

These are **not Tags**, but are often mistaken for them:

- **Audience** — Trait; can let the character seek help from their audience.
- **Connections** — Trait; provides mechanical access to contacts.
- **Signature Attack** — Trait; provides an Edge with a specified attack type.
- **Scientific Expertise** — Trait; provides an Edge on appropriate scientific research checks.
- **Public Speaking** — Trait; provides an Edge on appropriate Ego checks involving groups.
- **Situational Awareness** — Trait; improves initiative checks.
- **Sneaky** — Trait; provides an Edge for appropriate stealth checks.
- **Tech Reliance** — Trait; has specific consequences when the character is incapacitated.
- **Weakness** — Trait; creates a specific vulnerability with direct mechanical consequences.

The game's rules distinguish Traits from Tags explicitly: Traits have mechanics, while Tags generally do not. citeturn0search0turn2view0

# Player checklist

When adding a Tag:

1. Write the Tag and its qualifier when one exists.
2. Do **not** add a numerical bonus unless another rule explicitly references that Tag.
3. Check whether an expansion rule uses the Tag.
4. Define what the Tag means in the campaign.
5. Use Tags to establish contacts, resources, identity, history, vulnerabilities, and story hooks.
6. Remember that Tags can change during play.
7. Do not confuse a Tag with a similarly named Trait.

## Bottom line

**Tags describe what is true about the character; Traits and Powers describe what the character can mechanically do.**

When a Tag appears on a character sheet, ask:

> **“What rule references this Tag?”**

If no rule references it, there is normally **no number to add or subtract**. Its purpose is to establish a fact that the Narrator can incorporate into the story.
